# Experiment entry points

Current construction and maintenance code is grouped by purpose:

- `bilingual/`: grouped original needs, five-intent Chinese/English synthesis, full-original translation ablations, and frozen bilingual retrieval diagnostics.
- `metadata/`: publication intervals, paper citations and explicitly unknown individual contribution.
- `paper/`: separate English SIGIR main/supplement builds with artifact-backed or explicitly frozen result snapshots.
- `merit_factored_comparison.py` / `analyze_merit_factored.py`: complete paired native factorization ablation; a documented negative result, not the default method.

Frozen legacy experiment filenames remain at their original paths because stored protocol hashes, Slurm scripts and reproduction instructions refer to them. They are not separate production methods. Obsolete Markdown manuscripts and regenerable previews/bytecode have been removed. Further retirement must check protocol and import references, not just modification dates.

Do not delete raw inputs, active inference outputs, source provenance or failed scientific records as if they were caches. See `docs/usage.md` for commands and scope.

The exploratory `experiments.retrieval.cof_shortlist_ceiling` script computes relevance-label oracle ceilings for candidate pools on fully judged CoF SIGIR. For hard labels, 58 of 73 top-20 pools and nine top-50 pools have fewer than five positives; mean oracle P@5 is 0.608 and 0.962, respectively. The oracle uses test labels unavailable at inference and does not establish achievable accuracy or enterprise fit.

`experiments.retrieval.cof_field_union --output <new-directory>` explores a fixed 20-reviewer budget combining title and abstract candidate-LM rankings, filling remaining slots from the complete query, then reranking by BM25. On the reused CoF SIGIR test set, hard P@5 is 0.170 versus 0.156 for full-query top-20, with a paired query-bootstrap difference interval of [-0.005, 0.033]. Hard pool recall is 0.100 for both to three decimals. One timing per query excludes index construction, initialization, evidence selection and reasoning. These post-hoc results do not establish improvement, and reviewer matching is not enterprise fit.

`experiments.retrieval.cof_field_union_kdd --output <new-directory>` tests a 10-candidate version on 174 KDD queries, restricting retrieval to each query's 20 already-judged reviewers. In the 148 queries with a hard positive, mean pool recall is 0.568 for full-query top-10 and 0.569 for the field union; the paired difference interval is [-0.030, 0.033]. Single-query mean times are 1.67 and 3.35 ms, excluding model construction and initialization. This judged-candidate restriction is benchmark supervision, not a deployable global retrieval test. Label-oracle ceilings are not achievable ranking accuracy, and near-duplicate leakage remains possible.

`experiments.analyze_evidencebench_set_efficiency --output <new-directory>` rechecks saved EvidenceBench development outputs, their frozen dataset and native-candidate hashes, and coverage calculations. Across 37 attempts, set selection and independent Decisions obtain macro aspect recall 0.590 and 0.562, and micro recall 0.523 and 0.502. The two failed set attempts retain the semantic fallback. Recorded mean input tokens are 7,190 and 14,114, respectively, but fields and API interfaces differ; historical sequential timings do not establish a speedup. This is not a SetR checkpoint reproduction, expert-fit assessment, independent test, or semantic-support validation.

The two field-union experiments prepare CoF using its pinned upstream archive. SIGIR builds its own unique-paper lexical index in the new output directory; KDD uses the audit manifest generated from that dataset. They do not require a prior local experiment or a private corpus. In a local replay, all per-query metrics match the earlier runs (73 SIGIR and 174 KDD queries); build time is reported separately. An existing output directory is never overwritten.

`experiments.retrieval.cof_owned_vote_quality --output <new-directory>` compares candidate-50 max and vote aggregation against global lexical max and vote on the same owner-specific CoF SIGIR corpus. On 73 reused test queries, candidate max/vote soft P@5 is 0.395/0.359 and hard P@5 is 0.192/0.195. The paired vote-minus-max hard P@5 interval is [-0.033, 0.036]; it does not establish an improvement. Candidate max/vote mean times in one run are 18.28/18.17 ms, versus 16.70/16.69 ms globally. Index build, initialization and reasoning are excluded. Per-owner copies change document frequencies, reviewer matching is not industrial fit, and the intervals are post-hoc and unadjusted for multiple comparisons.

`experiments.evidencebench_deadline_coverage --output <new-directory>` replays time-integrated aspect coverage on the 37 development cases. It adds the same saved coarse-query time to each arm's historical API request time, and accounts for two failed set calls by returning the semantic fallback after the request. At a post-hoc five-second horizon the means are 0.254 (independent Decisions) and 0.332 (set selection); at ten seconds they are 0.403 and 0.459. These reconstructed, nonrandomized historical timings exclude initialization, index construction and other overhead. Aspect labels measure evidence coverage rather than expert fit or entailment; the post-hoc query-bootstrap intervals do not validate a deployment SLA or a SetR reproduction.

`experiments.retrieval.temporal_vote_vs_max --output <new-directory>` compares the reusable candidate-50 voting and maximum paths on the 200-query local historical-author proxy. Max/vote recover a known author for 95/80 queries. The paired vote-minus-max hit-rate difference is -0.075, with author-connected cluster-bootstrap interval [-0.126, -0.025]. All rankings match prior runs, but the cohort was repeatedly examined and the author associations are incomplete and unverified. These findings do not measure industrial expert fit, semantic support, or a latency distribution; source data are private.

`experiments.retrieval.temporal_profile_volume --output <new-directory>` audits saved max/vote rankings on the private historical proxy. Across 200 queries, the mean number of historical source records per returned expert is 17.2 for max and 29.8 for vote; vote is higher in 199 query-level comparisons. This observational difference does not establish why rankings differ. Source-record volume is not verified publication count, individual contribution, honorary authorship or technical relevance.

`experiments.retrieval.temporal_mean_evidence --output <new-directory>` explores dividing the top-five positive source-score sum by the number of positive matches, within the same fixed 50-candidate pool. On 200 repeatedly studied historical-author queries, mean aggregation returns experts with 17.7 historical records on average versus 17.2 for max and 29.8 for unnormalized vote; known-author hits are 90, 95 and 80 respectively. Mean-minus-max hit-rate difference is -0.025 with an author-cluster interval [-0.065, 0.015]. This exploratory heuristic does not establish attribution or enterprise accuracy. The three arms share retrieval and scoring, so their combined timing is not a per-method latency comparison. Local source data are private.
