\section{Protocols, Metadata, and Reproducibility}
\label{app:protocols}
\subsection{Ownership and source views}
Every view binds a query ID, expert ID, source ID, text hash, and character interval. The raw corpus retains full field text, while evaluation sees only frozen ranges. Expanding a source silently changes the labeled task. Ownership checks establish consistency with supplied associations, not real-world authorship or individual contribution. Source frames have globally unique keys formed from source ID and local frame ID. Repeated IDs, stale hashes, foreign quotations, and unadmitted cached frames are rejected. A null field means unreported, never impossible.

The 100-source stratified, query-blind frame pilot contains 40 papers and 15 each of projects, patents, biographies, and research-field lists, all from development sources. It admits 26 frames from 23 papers. Nineteen frames have documented inputs, 24 outputs, and 16 conditions; 45 quotations are exactly bound. All 100 sources remain raw-retrieval eligible. No frame survived screening in the 60 profile fields; sampled fields were often titles, broad areas, or publication counts. This does not imply lack of expert methods, and compilation itself can miss evidence. The pilot is not a complete searchable frame index or an amortization result.

\subsection{Publication and contribution metadata}
Original paper records number 54,409. Of these, 53,007 have parseable dates: 30,827 year-, 4,450 month-, and 17,730 day-resolution dates. There are 48,657 known citation totals, 53,750 author-list strings, and 8,034 DOIs. Explicit personal-contribution roles are absent. At a 2025-12-31 cutoff, 2,616 publications fall after the cutoff, 50,391 before/on it, and 1,402 have unknown dates. Year-only intervals overlapping a cutoff are not fabricated into exact dates. Current citation totals have no observation timestamps, so historical citation scoring is unsupported.

The sidecar preserves dates, raw author-list text, paper-level counts, and unknown personal credit without rewriting frozen source vectors. Author order, supervision, or high citations alone do not identify honorary authorship. Future verification must distinguish identity association, explicit method contribution, project responsibilities, and team-level evidence. Field- and age-normalized impact cannot be claimed when the required metadata are unavailable.

\subsection{Synthesis and bilingual controls}

An order-controlled audit of 30 selected real cases shows that the original joint grounding question is sensitive to presentation: 71 of 120 choice-order comparisons disagree. A later single-view instruction, with different inputs and target, yields 3 and 4 disagreements among 60 views in two sequential rounds; five of 120 same-order answers change between rounds. These are unjudged cases and the designs differ, so neither accuracy nor a causal prompt improvement follows. Simple single-view synthetic controls pass all tested choice orders but do not establish real-data reliability. A separate blind review package contains 30 unjudged cases, six per intent type.

Construction-screen coverage differs across intent types. Of 1,000 pairs per type, 115 component-method, 74 conditional-transfer, 279 constraint-focused, 212 evidence-lookup and 96 overview pairs remain unjudged. Filtering to screened pairs therefore changes the type distribution. Five original needs have no retained pairs, while 550 have all five. These are screen-coverage counts, not human error rates. A separate local review packet samples ten cases from each type and screening-status stratum, hides screen status, and currently contains no completed human judgments. Uncertain and missing judgments remain unknown; population-weighted sample intervals must not be mistaken for confidence intervals.

We separately retrieve paper and profile candidates and fuse their top-50 lists with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF). Exact fusion increases mean bilingual overlap@10 from 0.4216 to 0.4961 across the same 1,000 construction pairs; interleaving reaches 0.4644. Both channel shortlists contain 83.34\% of RRF output slots versus 33.64\% for interleaving. Approximate RRF retains 99.545\% of exact fused top-ten entries in 13.52\,ms per view, excluding encoding, loading, and index construction. Relevance and end-to-end superiority require independent evaluation.
Raw enterprise data contain 4,301 nonempty needs. The new company-grouped cohort excludes 136 prior need IDs and 1,424 additional records sharing exact prior-company keys, leaving 2,741 records in 1,395 connected groups. Category counts are 1,732 engineering, 235 agriculture, 253 humanities, 220 medicine, and 301 unspecified. The largest group has 54 records; actual splits are 2,228/284/229, not exact nominal percentages. Exact company keys do not resolve all subsidiary or semantic family aliases.

New duplicate normalization preserves decimal punctuation, inequality direction, and case-sensitive units. Lossless segments cover every original character with exact offsets. Arabic-number, supported-unit, and comparator checks reject some detectable translation changes but cannot certify all quantities or semantics. Scientifically equivalent reformatting can also trigger a conservative mismatch. Complete-parent English translations and expert-fit labels remain pending. At \SnapshotUTC{}, terminal records contain \SyntheticGenerated{} generated bilingual pairs, \SyntheticScreened{} current-screen passes, and \SyntheticUnjudged{} unjudged pairs from \ParentJobsDone{} parents. These counts are refreshed from selected-parent terminal records at build time. Five source-grounded intent slots per 1,000 construction parents target 5,000 derived queries with bilingual views; the 32-parent pilot yields 160 pairs and 136 model-screened pairs. These counts do not imply human-verified semantic equivalence. The separate version-2 postcheck recognizes additional negated comparators and marks unsupported scoped negation uncertain. It retains \PostRetained{} previous passes and downgrades \PostDowngraded{}; it does not promote earlier failures. Scientifically equivalent quantity reformatting and complete semantics remain outside its guarantee. Among 4,224 retained pairs, 3,355 have no numeric anchor detected in either language, so the numeric check provides no numeric evidence for 79.4\% of retained pairs; 869 have detected anchors in both views. Detection itself is incomplete and is not a semantic-quality label. The existing 100-case stratified human-review packet includes 43 such cases, but its evidence-lookup/unjudged stratum includes none of 22 eligible no-anchor cases. A fixed-seed, five-case local targeted supplement addresses this coverage gap; it is not part of the original probability sample, and neither packet has completed human judgments. A separate English non-numeric control uses eight handwritten source templates, each with an identical faithful query and a changed query; reversing choice order gives 32 Decisions calls, all matching their constructed labels. This small, dependent control lacks faithful paraphrases, Chinese cases, and independent human judgments, and cannot estimate real-data quality. Follow-up cohorts replace identical faithful queries with English paraphrases or Chinese translations against the English source; both return the intended label in all 32 calls. They use the same eight hand-authored templates, sequential runs, and only one cross-language direction; real-data accuracy and independent human agreement remain unmeasured. A post-hoc repair pilot of 16 previously studied full-original translations retains all three previously screened cases but promotes none of the nine other completed repairs; four cases remain unresolved. Repair uses a whole-text numeric check instead of the direct run's segment checks. These same-model screens do not establish equivalence or justify bulk adoption. A subsequent critique-and-repair trial selects the nine completed unjudged repairs: one passes the same-model and numeric screen, seven remain unjudged, and one is unresolved. This selected, repeatedly judged cohort is not independent evidence of translation quality. Variant and language-view counts must not be presented as independent original needs.

Earlier same-author intervention sets contain five and eight pairs with overlapping need/expert identities. They test support for narrow historical facts versus unknown evidence, not actual inability. The fresh lexical expansion adds 742 unjudged views involving 455 experts and 1,158 sources; 1,839 source-view checks preserve ownership and ranges. New snapshots do not inherit old labels.

\subsection{Local dataset export and status accounting}
The completed generation run supplies 1,000 original parents and 5,000 generated intent pairs. The local export stores original full Chinese needs separately from derived bilingual requests. Original records retain domain category, track, group ID, split, and spreadsheet file/row/text hashes. Derived records retain parent ID, intent type, both language strings, cited segment IDs, and final construction status. Each has a null expert-fit grade. The 4,224 retained screen passes and 776 unjudged pairs remain in the same export, so filtering decisions cannot erase the denominator.

Export checks the frozen original-query hash, each terminal parent hash against the post-validation manifest, parent identity, construction-split membership, and unique planned query IDs. It requires current postchecks for every generated variant. Missing text would remain an unresolved slot rather than count as a generated query. The final manifest records the code and input hashes, per-output hashes, taxonomy, generated count, and status counts. These invariants establish traceability and accounting; they do not certify semantic quality. The local data card records the remaining full-parent translation and relevance-annotation gaps. Public software includes the construction and checking code but excludes original enterprise text.

A release-readiness literal audit finds one generated Chinese view containing its complete parent company field after case and whitespace normalization. Exact 16-character parent runs appear in 1,073 Chinese and 45 English views, while 32-character runs appear in 39 and one respectively. Grounded technical rewriting can intentionally reuse source phrases; literal overlap alone neither proves an identity disclosure nor settles redistribution rights. The audit does not detect translated names, aliases, indirect identifiers or semantic paraphrases. A Unicode scan also finds Han characters or radicals in 33 English views, including 18 model-screened pairs. A repair pass and one unjudged follow-up produce a local 5,000-row English view with 5,000 Han-free texts; 4,221 rows retain model-screened status and 779 are unjudged. Twenty-one texts still trigger identifier rules. A local de-identification pilot produces 18 same-model-screened candidates and three unjudged candidates; 11 still trigger broad rules. A 21-case blinded paired review is prepared without human judgments. A local candidate view applies 18 screened edits, keeps three unresolved originals and leaves 4,979 rows unassessed; the broad rule count decreases from 21 to 13. This is not an anonymity estimate. A stratified sample of 200 previously unflagged rows receives no explicit-identity flags from the same model; a blinded human-review packet is prepared, but indirect identification and rights remain unassessed. A validator preserves missing and uncertain labels and reports weighted sample and finite-population worst-case bounds. With the blank packet both span $[0,1]$; they are not confidence intervals or anonymity evidence. A subsequent same-model Chinese--English recheck of the 18 edited candidates finds 16 equivalent in both versions and two disagreements. Downgrading the latter gives a local view with 4,219 screened and 781 unjudged rows; no human semantic labels are implied. For the nine English texts actually changed by de-identification, a post-hoc lexical index check finds 0.856 old/new top-ten overlap; Chinese versus old/new overlaps are 0.033/0.022. This small selected cohort measures stability, not relevance or bilingual semantic correctness. On six post-hoc corrected synthetic control pairs, both model checks match 12 constructed labels. A separate same-model review labels two original texts as identifiable and their candidates as not identifiable, and the remaining 19 pairs as neither; these are not human or privacy guarantees. Same-model checks and lexical scans do not establish semantic quality or safe release. Generated queries remain local pending source-rights assessment and review.

The 32-parent complete-translation ablations produce 78 complete translations across 96 attempts. A local model-blind review packet retains these 78 outputs, while the 18 incomplete attempts remain in their original denominators. No human judgments are yet available. In the direct arm, seven full translations pass all numeric checks but receive a changed-or-incomplete native judgment; four native-faithful outputs have numeric mismatch or uncertainty. These checks disagree and neither is human gold, so improving numeric formatting alone cannot establish faithful translation.

On 16 previously inspected development needs, a later context-preserving segment-by-segment translation completes 16 outputs versus 15 for direct translation, but passes the combined same-model/numeric screen for two versus three. Mean per-parent task durations are 11.62 versus 8.13 seconds under sequential experiments with three concurrent workers. Different call counts, temporal order, reused needs and absent human judgments preclude a quality or general speed claim. A separate 31-output blind-review packet remains entirely unjudged.

\subsection{Budget and interface comparisons}
We additionally release a small temporal OpenAlex author-recovery proxy built from CC0 publication metadata: 867 historical works, 2,593 candidate author IDs, and 99 eligible held-out title queries. Five topical API searches and source/response hashes are recorded. Exact matches and high character-four-gram-overlap historical titles are excluded using a threshold chosen after inspecting a preliminary snapshot; semantic leakage and author disambiguation remain unaudited. Only target-paper authors with historical evidence are known positives; other candidates are unjudged, and 792 target works without an eligible author are excluded. BM25-max and BM25-vote recover at least one known author in 21 and 27 of 99 queries, respectively. This is a reproducible public proxy, not enterprise expert-fit, individual contribution or full-corpus recall. A separate rotating-order retrieval comparison on the same 99 queries gives 20/99 hits for candidate language-model ranking with selective evidence lookup, compared with 27/99 for BM25-vote. Mean one-shot times are 0.81 and 5.77 milliseconds respectively, excluding initialization and model reasoning; candidate index construction adds 0.18 seconds after a shared 0.04-second lexical build. This small proxy indicates a speed--recovery trade-off, not quality equivalence or scalable end-to-end latency. A later post-hoc control reranks the top 50 candidate-LM authors by the sum of up to five BM25 evidence scores: 25/99 hits and known-author recall 0.242, versus 27/99 and 0.256 for precomputed BM25-vote. Precomputed and ordinary BM25-vote have identical ordered top-ten lists for all 99 queries. Their mean one-shot retrieval times are 1.22 and 2.34 milliseconds, respectively, excluding initialization, construction, and model calls. These are development observations on reused data, not a held-out speed--quality guarantee. A later candidate-reasoning revision requires an exact quote in each cited owned source and records offsets within the visible text. A four-query local integration pilot completed all calls and returned 12 quote-anchored results, with two known-author hits; mean query time was 12.69 seconds. This verifies localization and integration only, not source entailment, identity, personal credit, or a comparison with the earlier ID-only prompt. A same-model support auditor marks all 12 reasons unsupported, both using excerpts and full visible source text. This result is unreliable: in a later control with five simple supported and five unsupported examples under two choice orders, it marks all 20 unsupported, including all ten supported instances. The auditor cannot be used as an automatic correctness filter without further validation. A later 2-by-2 instruction and choice-set wording ablation on the same controls returns five supported, four uncertain and one unsupported for ten positive instances in its best arm, and marks all ten negative instances unsupported. Choice values and descriptions change together, so individual wording effects are not isolated. These post-hoc variants remain unsuitable for automatic filtering. On six new handcrafted template pairs, the revised Decisions choice set and explained Chat each recognize three of six supported controls. Decisions rejects the other three; Chat marks them uncertain. Decisions rejects all six unsupported controls, while Chat rejects five and leaves one uncertain. These dependent examples do not validate either auditor. A later atomic prompt omitting query and attribution constraints matches all 12 labels on these reused controls; a contemporaneous scoped prompt matches 11. The scoped positive count changes across runs and slight prompt differences. Atomic entailment does not establish demand relevance or personal contribution, and these post-hoc results do not validate filtering. A same-model exploratory decomposition of 12 prior recommendations extracts 44 exact reason substrings: 17 source facts, eight transfer inferences, one personal-role claim and 18 others. Against cited excerpts, three source facts receive yes and 14 no; repeating the same claims with full visible source changes six of 17 judgments. The native atomic question matches all 12 reused simple controls, but decomposition completeness, batch behavior, transfer and personal attribution are unverified. A literal coverage check finds that extracted substrings cover 94.9\% of non-punctuation, non-whitespace characters overall, but fully cover only two of 12 reasons; the subset categorized as source facts covers 45.6\%. This character statistic does not establish semantic coverage or correctness. A stage audit finds at least one known author in 31 top-50 shortlists and 25 reranked top tens; four of the 27 full-BM25-hit queries have no known author in the shortlist, while two shortlist-reranked hits are full-BM25 misses. Comparisons are at query level and may involve different known authors.

For a prespecified horizon $T$, we additionally summarize the latest complete-result gain bounds over time as $T^{-1}\int_0^T L(R(t))\,dt$ and $T^{-1}\int_0^T H(R(t))\,dt$. The empty result has zero gain before completion, and later snapshots replace earlier ones even after a regression. This is an ordinary area-under-curve application, not a new metric theorem. Bounds address unjudged positions conditional on the supplied labels; they do not capture sampling uncertainty, annotator error, or actual expert ability. A post-hoc application to the 20-query output-budget cohort uses a ten-second request-time horizon: focused-minus-full shared-label average bounds are $[-0.0016,0.2153]$, versus $[-0.2245,0.4382]$ from separately bounded arms. Focused and full return 20 and 18 completed results by the deadline, respectively. The horizon was chosen after observing the run, retrieval and initialization are excluded, and the labels are incomplete same-model silver; neither interval establishes an accuracy difference. Compare systems at the same horizon and report quality, completion and latency separately. No independently labeled enterprise result is available for this summary. On all 80 development needs, Qwen paper-max versus BM25-max has shared-label bounds $[-0.241,0.295]$; 50 high-impact unjudged views have been packaged for blind review, with no human labels yet. A later full-need retrieval-only audit averages 58.6 ms for candidate-LM evidence lookup and 254.7 ms for adaptive candidate-50 scoring; these single observations exclude initialization and reasoning. A paired later run accumulates only candidate scores during global scans and preserves all 80 results, averaging 233.3 versus 197.8 ms. An optional resident sorted-postings index preserves all 80 results and averages 16.2 versus 213.7 ms in a separate paired replay; building it takes 16.8 seconds and its numeric arrays occupy 104.8 MB, excluding dictionary overhead. It fails closed on observed external database changes. In a four-need agent pilot, the same-prompt arms each succeed three times and time out once; mean attempt times are 25.83 and 25.84 seconds, with initialization excluded.

\subsection{Human-self-rated benchmark}
Official MERIT assets are pinned to commit \path{5fad0ea06148c3c8817aac841de46c98a75eb40c}. Nine selected files total 20,021,659 bytes and match upstream Git blob hashes, lengths, and SHA-256 values. Only paper title/abstract/introduction and candidate publication title/abstract enter inference; rating fields are read only during evaluation. The long arm uses the authors' released prompt without running their trained checkpoint. Short and native arms share its core-plus-additional-skill criterion, with distinct transport and output formats. This is a practical interface/prompt comparison, not identical internal computation.

All 1,830 requests completed validly. Full reasoning has TP/FP/TN/FN of 176/85/271/78; short Chat 223/160/196/31; native choice 180/102/254/74. Empirical P95 request times are 4.913, 2.441, and 1.608 seconds, respectively. Native-minus-short accuracy is $+2.46$ points with interval $[-0.81,5.79]$, not a demonstrated gain. The analysis refuses partial cohorts and supports explicit abstention bounds, though none were needed here. Bootstrap uses 509 exact target-title groups, not verified reviewer identities or semantic-duplicate clusters. Self-rated expertise and enterprise method transfer remain distinct tasks. In a later post-hoc replay, exact-title hashing divides these already studied pairs into 290 development and 320 test cases. A development-selected maximum-choice-probability threshold of 0.75 escalates 141 test cases to stored full reasoning. Test accuracy is 76.88\%, versus 76.25\% for full reasoning; the paired paper-cluster interval for their difference is $[-0.98,2.44]$ points. Simulated serial mean time is 2.54 versus 3.80 seconds using previously observed concurrent request times. This is not a measured cascade, a noninferiority claim, or an untouched benchmark.
