# SciFact source-conditioned verdict pilot

The [official SciFact task](https://github.com/allenai/scifact/blob/master/doc/data.md) distinguishes whether an annotated abstract supports or contradicts an expert-written claim, with one or more rationale sentence sets. Its original development labels and abstracts are separate from the BEIR document-retrieval adaptation already used by this project. The [official evaluation](https://github.com/allenai/scifact/blob/master/doc/evaluation.md) also requires rationale and verdict checks; the pilot below does not reproduce that evaluation.

We selected the first 20 development claims per verdict by numeric claim ID, excluding claims whose annotated abstracts have mixed verdicts and taking the lowest numbered annotated abstract for each remaining claim. The model receives the claim and full title/abstract; it does not receive the verdict or rationale sentence indices. One native `gpt-6-luna` Decisions request per case chooses support, contradict, or unknown. No retriever, author information, or enterprise need is involved.

All 40 calls returned valid choices. Against the selected abstract-level labels, 18 of 20 support cases were classified as support, one as contradict and one as unknown; all 20 contradict cases were classified as contradict. Overall agreement is 38/40. Unknown is counted as incorrect in that figure. Mean request attempt time was 0.873 seconds with three concurrent workers, excluding retrieval; this is not an end-to-end latency estimate.

This nonrandom, balanced development pilot supplies an annotated abstract by construction. It does not test whether the system can retrieve it, select the annotated rationale, verify individual contributions, or solve industrial expert search. The sample is small, may overlap model pretraining, and has no independent assessment of label or source truth. A label mismatch can reflect model error, annotation limitations, or ambiguity; this pilot does not adjudicate the cause. Do not interpret the balanced accuracy as performance under natural prevalence.

The official archive downloaded from [AI2's published location](https://scifact.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/release/latest/data.tar.gz) has SHA-256 `11c621288d41ac144d29b13b0f8503b3820b7d6e8b1f6ff24dff335c196d76be`. It is kept locally; its data terms apply separately and are not changed by the software license. To reproduce the protocol, download that archive, check the hash, configure the model credentials, and run `PYTHONPATH=src:. python -m experiments.scifact.decisions_oracle_pilot --archive <archive> --output <new-directory>`. This makes 40 paid model calls; new model responses and timing can differ. The local protocol records selected IDs, choices, model, input hash and code hashes, with no source texts in its output.

## Cited abstracts without evidence annotations

A post-hoc follow-up excludes all 40 prior claims and selects the first 40 development claims with a cited corpus abstract absent from that claim's evidence annotations, choosing the lowest numeric abstract ID per claim. The same model and question are used. All 40 calls returned valid choices: 33 unknown, five contradict and two support. Mean attempt time was 0.754 seconds with three workers; this is not service latency.

The absence of a recorded rationale is not independently verified evidence that a claim is unsupported. The seven definite model judgments are therefore review targets, not measured false positives. This nonrandom sample does not measure natural prevalence, model accuracy, retrieval quality or industrial fit. The source snapshot and source-only selection were frozen before these calls.

A local packet contains all 40 claim/abstract views with the model outputs and annotation status hidden; source text can reveal identity. All 40 human judgments are currently missing. A reviewer should make an independent copy of `review.json`, enter `support`, `contradict`, or `unknown`, provide a reviewer identifier, and for support or contradiction supply an exact quote from one abstract sentence. Validate with `PYTHONPATH=src:. python -m experiments.scifact.check_unannotated_review --root <packet-directory> --review <review-copy>`. Exact quotation and fixed-view checks do not establish entailment or reviewer independence. Raw source text and the packet are not included in the software release.

## Oracle-abstract rationale selection

On the same 40 previously explored development examples, a later pilot compares top-three sentence selection within the already known evidence abstract. A local BM25-style sentence ranker is the baseline. A single native Decisions request labels every sentence as relevant, not relevant or uncertain; its yes-labeled sentences are returned in lexical order, capped at three, without filling from other labels. The claim and full abstract are shared context; original verdicts and rationale indices are withheld.

All 40 model calls completed. Both arms cover at least one complete annotated rationale for 37/40 examples and have annotated-sentence P@3 of 0.433. Their ordered selected sentence lists agree on 31 examples and differ on nine; equal aggregate metrics do not mean identical selections. The lexical selector averages 0.923 ms of local processing, while the model attempt averages 1.161 seconds using three concurrent workers. This comparison excludes retrieval and initialization, and the timings are not production service latency.

This is a post-hoc oracle-abstract diagnostic, not the official joint verdict-and-rationale metric. Rationale annotations may be incomplete, the two methods have different selection rules, and the sample is small and reused. The result provides no evidence that the model screen increases coverage or efficiency, and no conclusion about enterprise expert fit or personal contribution. Reproduce with `PYTHONPATH=src:. python -m experiments.scifact.rationale_selection_pilot --archive <archive> --parent <oracle-pilot-directory> --output <new-directory>`; this makes 40 model calls.

## Retrieved-abstract pipeline diagnostic

A subsequent run uses those same 40 development claims against the full adapted BEIR SciFact corpus. BM25 retrieves ten abstracts, the first is selected, the local sentence ranker returns up to three sentences, and native Decisions classifies the full retrieved abstract. Adapted query text and indexed source ownership are checked; the selected source is compared to the official abstract after whitespace normalization. The model sees neither labels nor rationale indices.

For 31/40 claims, the top-one abstract has an upstream evidence annotation; 39/40 have at least one annotated abstract in the top ten. Among those 31 top-one annotated cases, all 31 source verdicts agree with the corresponding annotation, 29 include a complete annotated rationale in the three lexical sentences, and the same 29 satisfy both conditions. The nine unannotated top-one abstracts are not verified negatives, so 29/40 is an observed annotated joint-hit count, not a claim that the other 11 are all factually wrong or a full precision estimate. The verdict model sees the full abstract rather than just the selected sentences, so joint hits do not establish that its verdict follows from the displayed sentence subset. This diagnostic is not the official SciFact scorer.

Mean local top-ten retrieval and sentence selection were 17.19 ms and 0.321 ms, respectively; mean model attempt was 0.742 seconds with three workers. The separate stages have different concurrency and exclude index build, initialization and queueing; they do not establish end-to-end service latency. The sample was reused and balanced after prior inspection. It is scientific claim verification, not industrial expert matching or individual contribution attribution. Run `PYTHONPATH=src:. python -m experiments.scifact.retrieval_verdict_pilot --archive <archive> --parent <oracle-pilot-directory> --corpus <adapted-corpus> --database <compiled-index> --output <new-directory>` after preparing the documented BEIR adapter and original archive; this makes 40 model calls.

## Verdicts from displayed sentences

A sequential follow-up reuses the 40 retrieved top-one abstracts and the three lexical sentences from the preceding run. It sends the same title and claim but only the selected sentences, ordered as in the original abstract, and uses the same Decisions question. The model is not shown the previous verdict, rationale annotations, or evidence status. All 40 new calls and their earlier counterparts were valid. Verdicts match in 35/40 cases: four previously definite verdicts become unknown, and one support becomes contradict.

Among the 31 cases with an annotated top-one abstract, 31 full-abstract judgments and 27 selected-sentence judgments match the abstract label. The selected-minus-full paired difference is -0.129, with post-hoc, unadjusted query-bootstrap interval [-0.258, -0.032]. The 27 selected-sentence matches also contain a complete annotated rationale. The other nine retrieved abstracts lack annotations and are not counted as wrong. Labels apply to the full abstract; correctness or agreement does not establish that the selected text alone entails the verdict. Calls ran at different times, and the shorter view also changes sentence ordering relative to the selector's ranked output, so response variability and presentation are confounded with source length. The small reused sample and possible pretraining overlap limit generalization. Mean new attempt time was 0.900 seconds with three workers, excluding retrieval; it is not an end-to-end or causal speed comparison. Reproduce with `PYTHONPATH=src:. python -m experiments.scifact.selected_sentence_verdict_pilot --archive <archive> --parent <retrieval-pilot-directory> --output <new-directory>`; this makes 40 model calls.
