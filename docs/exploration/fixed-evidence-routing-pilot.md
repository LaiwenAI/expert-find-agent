# Fixed-evidence routing pilot

Two held-out-title author-recovery proxy queries were run with the same lexical candidate evidence pool per query. The direct arm ranked all 60 available evidence entries in one model call. The routed arm used a model call to select up to 30 entries, followed by a ranking call. The inputs were hashed and the arm order alternated. All calls returned `gpt-6-luna`; initialization and candidate retrieval are excluded from the arm timings.

| Query | Direct seconds | Routed seconds | Direct / routed evidence | Direct / routed results | Known author found |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 16.24 | 17.02 | 60 / 4 | 10 / 3 | Both |
| 2 | 87.05 | 32.33 | 60 / 15 | 10 / 10 | Direct only |

For the second query, the known author was in the candidate pool, with two of three available evidence entries selected by routing. The author was absent from the model's raw ranking, not removed by citation ownership validation. This does not establish why the model omitted the author. The two queries are too few to infer a latency or quality advantage; model calls have high variance, known authors are incomplete relevance judgments, and author recovery is not industrial expert fit. The evidence-pool hash does not prove equal evidence visibility at ranking time, since selection is the intervention. Local records are in `artifacts/fixed-evidence-routing-pilot-v1/`.

A post-hoc coverage-constrained run on the second query used the same hashed candidate pool, selected 24 evidence items covering all 20 candidates, and returned the known author among ten results. It took 87.85 seconds. The route was regenerated and the run occurred later, so this single observation cannot isolate the coverage constraint from model or temporal variation; the case was chosen after observing the earlier omission. Local output is in `artifacts/routing-coverage-pilot-v1/`. A prospective paired evaluation on separate queries is needed before treating coverage as beneficial.

Two further proxy queries shared one frozen route and candidate pool between the unconstrained and coverage-constrained arms. Their evidence counts were 2 versus 21 and 5 versus 21; known authors returned were 1 versus 2 and 1 versus 1. Ranking-call times were 5.06 versus 12.93 seconds and 12.13 versus 22.81 seconds. The shared routing calls cost another 5.66 and 9.78 seconds, respectively. Arm order alternated, but there was only one call per arm and only two queries; the extra evidence and model stochasticity both change the output. These observations do not establish an accuracy or latency benefit. Local outputs are in `artifacts/frozen-route-coverage-pilot-v1/`.

On four subsequent proxy queries, a one-call coverage baseline retained the first retrieved evidence item for each candidate, while the direct baseline exposed all retrieved evidence. Both arms shared candidate-pool hashes and alternated order; all eight calls were valid and returned `gpt-6-luna`. Direct used 58--60 evidence entries and averaged 17.10 seconds; coverage used 20 and averaged 10.87 seconds, excluding retrieval and initialization. Both returned the known author for each query, but the known author's rank was 1 in all direct runs and 1, 2, 1, 1 in coverage runs. Output sizes and other ranked experts also differed. Four calls per arm do not establish noninferiority, independent industrial relevance or a reliable latency distribution. Local outputs are in `artifacts/direct-coverage-paired-pilot-v1/`.

An additional eight proxy queries used the same two arms and alternating order. All 16 attempts succeeded and each paired input hash matched. Direct and coverage mean ranking times were 19.26 and 15.26 seconds; mean MRR@10 against incomplete known-author labels was 0.375 and 0.389. Coverage was faster on seven of eight queries. An exploratory query bootstrap gives a mean time difference of -4.00 seconds with percentile interval [-7.03, -1.04]; one timing per arm/query does not capture repeat variation. Only one query changed MRR. These are development proxy queries and do not establish noninferiority, real expert relevance, or end-to-end speed. Local outputs and frozen protocol are in `artifacts/direct-coverage-paired-v2/`.

The original agent accumulated zero when an API response omitted token usage, without recording whether usage was present. These historical records therefore cannot establish complete token accounting, even where totals are present. New traces explicitly record usage presence; the analysis omits token means unless every attempt has verified usage. Timing and ranking records remain separate observations.

## Enterprise development pilot

Eight lexicographically selected development queries from real enterprise requirements were compared prospectively using the same frozen seven-candidate pool and source snapshots. Direct ranking saw all available snapshot entries; coverage ranking saw the first entry per candidate. Thus the shared input hash verifies the common pool, not identical evidence visibility. The 16 calls succeeded, used `gpt-6-luna`, and alternated arm order. The one-call ranking times averaged 26.27 seconds for direct and 17.68 seconds for coverage, excluding retrieval and initialization. Usage was present for every call: mean prompt tokens were 5,840 and 2,173, and mean completion tokens were 1,733 and 1,372, respectively. These are small-sample observations with one call per arm and query, not an end-to-end latency guarantee.

The paired difference in actionable gain at three, coverage minus direct, lies within [-0.133, 0.074] under the stated independent unknown-label box. This is an identification interval, not a confidence interval: observed grades are incomplete same-model silver labels, not independent expert judgments. They describe relevance to the full common snapshot, and cannot establish that an arm's displayed evidence supports its citations. The interval includes both a loss and a gain; the pilot cannot establish higher precision or noninferiority. The lexicographic development sample is not representative. Local frozen inputs and results are in `artifacts/enterprise-coverage-pilot-v1/`; the analysis is reproducible with `python -m experiments.retrieval.analyze_enterprise_coverage artifacts/enterprise-coverage-pilot-v1` with `src` on `PYTHONPATH`. The original run predates code-hash recording, so its protocol does not establish the exact executed source revision.

As a separate implementation check, batching selected evidence-body reads in the compiled lexical index produced identical complete search outputs on 20 existing local benchmark queries. Alternating old and new calls averaged 0.311 and 0.263 seconds, respectively, excluding initialization; each query was timed once. This exploratory check has no saved per-query timing record, does not measure the model or complete pipeline, and cannot establish a general speedup. It is an implementation optimization rather than a new retrieval method.
