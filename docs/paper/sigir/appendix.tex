\section{Protocols, Metadata, and Reproducibility}
\label{app:protocols}
\subsection{Ownership and source views}
Every view binds a query ID, expert ID, source ID, text hash, and character interval. The raw corpus retains full field text, while evaluation sees only frozen ranges. Expanding a source silently changes the labeled task. Ownership checks establish consistency with supplied associations, not real-world authorship or individual contribution. Source frames have globally unique keys formed from source ID and local frame ID. Repeated IDs, stale hashes, foreign quotations, and unadmitted cached frames are rejected. A null field means unreported, never impossible.

The 100-source stratified, query-blind frame pilot contains 40 papers and 15 each of projects, patents, biographies, and research-field lists, all from development sources. It admits 26 frames from 23 papers. Nineteen frames have documented inputs, 24 outputs, and 16 conditions; 45 quotations are exactly bound. All 100 sources remain raw-retrieval eligible. No frame survived screening in the 60 profile fields; sampled fields were often titles, broad areas, or publication counts. This does not imply lack of expert methods, and compilation itself can miss evidence. The pilot is not a complete searchable frame index or an amortization result.

\subsection{Publication and contribution metadata}
Original paper records number 54,409. Of these, 53,007 have parseable dates: 30,827 year-, 4,450 month-, and 17,730 day-resolution dates. There are 48,657 known citation totals, 53,750 author-list strings, and 8,034 DOIs. Explicit personal-contribution roles are absent. At a 2025-12-31 cutoff, 2,616 publications fall after the cutoff, 50,391 before/on it, and 1,402 have unknown dates. Year-only intervals overlapping a cutoff are not fabricated into exact dates. Current citation totals have no observation timestamps, so historical citation scoring is unsupported.

The sidecar preserves dates, raw author-list text, paper-level counts, and unknown personal credit without rewriting frozen source vectors. Author order, supervision, or high citations alone do not identify honorary authorship. Future verification must distinguish identity association, explicit method contribution, project responsibilities, and team-level evidence. Field- and age-normalized impact cannot be claimed when the required metadata are unavailable.

\subsection{Synthesis and bilingual controls}

An order-controlled audit of 30 selected real cases shows that the original joint grounding question is sensitive to presentation: 71 of 120 choice-order comparisons disagree. A later single-view instruction, with different inputs and target, yields 3 and 4 disagreements among 60 views in two sequential rounds; five of 120 same-order answers change between rounds. These are unjudged cases and the designs differ, so neither accuracy nor a causal prompt improvement follows. Simple single-view synthetic controls pass all tested choice orders but do not establish real-data reliability. A separate blind review package contains 30 unjudged cases, six per intent type.

Construction-screen coverage differs across intent types. Of 1,000 pairs per type, 115 component-method, 74 conditional-transfer, 279 constraint-focused, 212 evidence-lookup and 96 overview pairs remain unjudged. Filtering to screened pairs therefore changes the type distribution. Five original needs have no retained pairs, while 550 have all five. These are screen-coverage counts, not human error rates. A separate local review packet samples ten cases from each type and screening-status stratum, hides screen status, and currently contains no completed human judgments. Uncertain and missing judgments remain unknown; population-weighted sample intervals must not be mistaken for confidence intervals.

We separately retrieve paper and profile candidates and fuse their top-50 lists with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF). Exact fusion increases mean bilingual overlap@10 from 0.4216 to 0.4961 across the same 1,000 construction pairs; interleaving reaches 0.4644. Both channel shortlists contain 83.34\% of RRF output slots versus 33.64\% for interleaving. Approximate RRF retains 99.545\% of exact fused top-ten entries in 13.52\,ms per view, excluding encoding, loading, and index construction. Relevance and end-to-end superiority require independent evaluation.
Raw enterprise data contain 4,301 nonempty needs. The new company-grouped cohort excludes 136 prior need IDs and 1,424 additional records sharing exact prior-company keys, leaving 2,741 records in 1,395 connected groups. Category counts are 1,732 engineering, 235 agriculture, 253 humanities, 220 medicine, and 301 unspecified. The largest group has 54 records; actual splits are 2,228/284/229, not exact nominal percentages. Exact company keys do not resolve all subsidiary or semantic family aliases.

New duplicate normalization preserves decimal punctuation, inequality direction, and case-sensitive units. Lossless segments cover every original character with exact offsets. Arabic-number, supported-unit, and comparator checks reject some detectable translation changes but cannot certify all quantities or semantics. Scientifically equivalent reformatting can also trigger a conservative mismatch. Complete-parent English translations and expert-fit labels remain pending. At \SnapshotUTC{}, terminal records contain \SyntheticGenerated{} generated bilingual pairs, \SyntheticScreened{} current-screen passes, and \SyntheticUnjudged{} unjudged pairs from \ParentJobsDone{} parents. These counts are refreshed from selected-parent terminal records at build time. Five source-grounded intent slots per 1,000 construction parents target 5,000 derived queries with bilingual views; the 32-parent pilot yields 160 pairs and 136 model-screened pairs. These counts do not imply human-verified semantic equivalence. The separate version-2 postcheck recognizes additional negated comparators and marks unsupported scoped negation uncertain. It retains \PostRetained{} previous passes and downgrades \PostDowngraded{}; it does not promote earlier failures. Scientifically equivalent quantity reformatting and complete semantics remain outside its guarantee. Variant and language-view counts must not be presented as independent original needs.

Earlier same-author intervention sets contain five and eight pairs with overlapping need/expert identities. They test support for narrow historical facts versus unknown evidence, not actual inability. The fresh lexical expansion adds 742 unjudged views involving 455 experts and 1,158 sources; 1,839 source-view checks preserve ownership and ranges. New snapshots do not inherit old labels.

\subsection{Local dataset export and status accounting}
The completed generation run supplies 1,000 original parents and 5,000 generated intent pairs. The local export stores original full Chinese needs separately from derived bilingual requests. Original records retain domain category, track, group ID, split, and spreadsheet file/row/text hashes. Derived records retain parent ID, intent type, both language strings, cited segment IDs, and final construction status. Each has a null expert-fit grade. The 4,224 retained screen passes and 776 unjudged pairs remain in the same export, so filtering decisions cannot erase the denominator.

Export checks the frozen original-query hash, each terminal parent hash against the post-validation manifest, parent identity, construction-split membership, and unique planned query IDs. It requires current postchecks for every generated variant. Missing text would remain an unresolved slot rather than count as a generated query. The final manifest records the code and input hashes, per-output hashes, taxonomy, generated count, and status counts. These invariants establish traceability and accounting; they do not certify semantic quality. The local data card records the remaining full-parent translation and relevance-annotation gaps. Public software includes the construction and checking code but excludes original enterprise text.

\subsection{Budget and interface comparisons}
For a prespecified horizon $T$, we additionally summarize the latest complete-result gain bounds over time as $T^{-1}\int_0^T L(R(t))\,dt$ and $T^{-1}\int_0^T H(R(t))\,dt$. The empty result has zero gain before completion, and later snapshots replace earlier ones even after a regression. This is an ordinary area-under-curve application, not a new metric theorem. Bounds address unjudged positions conditional on the supplied labels; they do not capture sampling uncertainty, annotator error, or actual expert ability. Compare systems at the same horizon and report quality, completion and latency separately. No independently labeled enterprise result is available for this summary.

As an exploratory implementation check on 73 public SIGIR reviewer-matching queries, a post-hoc 30-second horizon yields mean integrated soft/hard gains of 0.209/0.099 for full evidence, 0.381/0.183 for similarity packing and 0.340/0.155 for coverage packing. These labels concern reviewer matching, not evidence support or enterprise fit. Summed observed component times include sequential shared rate-limit waits; order and previous requests affect them. Initialization and encoding are excluded, so these values are not a controlled end-to-end comparison or a significance claim. Failed requests retain the prior experiment's coarse fallback after the failed call.

In a separate eight-query author-recovery proxy pilot, a single ranking call using one retrieved evidence item per candidate averaged 15.26 seconds, versus 19.26 seconds when all retrieved evidence was visible. Both arms used the same candidate pools, alternated order, and completed all 16 calls. Their mean MRR@10 against incomplete known-author labels was 0.389 and 0.375. A query bootstrap of paired time differences gives $[-7.03,-1.04]$ seconds, excluding retrieval and initialization. One call per arm and eight proxy queries cannot establish industrial relevance, noninferiority, or a latency distribution. A separate four-query pilot found one known author's rank changed from first to second under coverage.

On eight lexicographically selected real enterprise development queries, the same comparison used seven frozen candidates per query and alternated arm order. All 16 calls succeeded. Direct versus coverage ranking averaged 26.27 versus 17.68 seconds, 5,840 versus 2,173 prompt tokens, and 1,733 versus 1,372 completion tokens; retrieval and initialization are excluded. Usage was present in every response. The paired coverage-minus-direct actionable gain at three lies in $[-0.133,0.074]$ under an independent unknown-label box. The incomplete labels are same-model silver grades for the full common snapshot, not independent expert judgments or verification that the evidence shown supports the returned citations. This identification interval is not a confidence interval and does not establish a quality advantage or noninferiority. Shared pool hashes do not imply identical evidence visibility. The original run did not record executed code hashes.

A separate four-query development pilot compares complete single-pass and tree-agent search paths, with different retrieval plans, evidence and prompts. All eight attempts succeed; mean time including retrieval and model calls is 19.93 versus 50.95 seconds, excluding initialization and index construction. Mean prompt tokens are 21,874 versus 24,350; completion tokens are 1,393 versus 3,056. The candidate sets share 7.5 of 20 IDs on average. One attempt per arm and query does not establish a latency distribution. The 37 returned results have no independent evidence-support judgments; a local blinded review packet is prepared. This comparison does not demonstrate precision or noninferiority.

Another four development queries compare complete versus one-item-per-candidate evidence on shared 20-expert lexical pools. All eight attempts succeed. Mean ranking times are 28.07 versus 18.27 seconds, and mean prompt tokens are 26,001 versus 11,465; shared retrieval averages 0.94 seconds and initialization is excluded. Their top-five sets share four expert IDs on average. All four pools were independently reconstructed from the frozen index. These unjudged development observations do not establish citation support, precision or noninferiority; a 40-result local blind-review packet is pending human judgments.

An optional candidate-first inverted index reduces evidence-lookup work after candidate selection. On 20 development needs, candidate-first and global-posting lookup return identical evidence for the same candidate-language-model shortlist. Single alternating calls average 0.057 versus 0.194 seconds, with shortlist ranking at 0.0076 seconds; startup and index building are excluded. The separate SQLite index copy grows from approximately 584 to 745 MiB. This is an engineering comparison on one machine, not an end-to-end or relevance-quality result. The combined prototype checks evidence bodies and ownership at startup, while a separate offline audit checks precomputed posting scores.

In a subsequent four-query development comparison with one model call per path, candidate-first search and reasoning averages 24.41 seconds versus 18.50 seconds for a lexical single-pass agent, including retrieval and excluding initialization. Mean prompt tokens are 11,154 versus 24,726; the two 20-candidate sets share five IDs on average. Despite lower prompt use, candidate-first is slower in this small sample. The paths differ in ranking and evidence budget, one call per query cannot establish latency variability or causation, and 37 returned results remain without independent evidence-support judgments. This experiment does not establish precision, noninferiority or end-to-end superiority.

Two additional attempts per path on the same four needs reverse the timing contrast: all 16 succeed and candidate-first versus lexical mean time is 17.57 versus 19.11 seconds. The paired mean difference is $-1.54$ seconds, compared with $+5.91$ seconds in the earlier pass. Across the two repeats, ordered outputs are identical for zero and one of four needs, respectively. These are repeated requests on the same needs, not additional independent queries; they show sensitivity to request variation without establishing a general speed advantage or relevance quality.
In development positions 0:20, a native gate retaining non-insufficient candidates reduces detailed comparisons from 140 to 91 but increases mean time from 5.64 to 7.09 seconds and omits one of 15 known silver positives. The staged-minus-full bootstrap interval is $[0.332,2.935]$ seconds. Failed gates pass all candidates; unselected candidates are not labeled incapable.

Positions 20:40 compare seven-explanation and top-three-only outputs on identical candidate data and the same 2,400-token cap. Valid outputs are 18/20 and 20/20. Ordered top-three agreement is 8/18, set overlap 87.0\%, and known silver positives returned are 8/9 and 9/9. Incomplete silver gain bounds remain broad. Same-model post-hoc witness screening rejects many proposed connections; its time is not charged to the measured online arms because it is a separate diagnostic. Online deployment requiring this audit must account for its additional cost.

Positions 40:60 add complete Chinese expression and explicit operation/compatibility/adaptation requirements together. Repeated arm-blind audits support 28 baseline and 40 modified claims in both runs. Missing first-run verdicts remain missing. Positions 60:80 isolate language and bridge factors in a four-arm Williams order, each order repeated five times. The language screen main effect has interval $[-0.0083,0.1833]$; the bridge interval is $[0.0333,0.1917]$, and the interaction is unresolved. Combining factors increases mean latency by 2.048 seconds, interval $[0.295,4.602]$. These are exploratory same-model behavioral contrasts, not independent industrial precision.

Bootstrap uses 509 exact target-title groups, not verified reviewer identities or semantic-duplicate clusters. Self-rated expertise and enterprise method transfer remain distinct tasks.

\subsection{Bilingual diagnostic execution}
Slurm job 39435 ran on ws6 and completed with exit code 0:0 in 2 minutes 51 seconds. Source/vector hashes and all official checkpoint-file hashes were checked on the compute node. Query vectors have shape $2,000\times1,024$, are finite and normalized, and their IDs match the frozen one-intent-per-parent selection. Source vectors stay on the compute disk. A separate Slurm CPU transfer packages only result files, logs, and query vectors; gzip CRC, permitted archive paths, and protocol/evaluation/vector hashes are checked locally.

CPU methods alternate order across query/language positions, use one FAISS thread, and receive one timed call per view after warmup. Their timings are exploratory observations rather than repeated-query latency estimates. A 2,000-replicate bootstrap resamples the 1,000 connected company groups. Exact language overlap is 0.4216; floor-64 and floor-512 overlap differences are $-0.0066$ and $-0.0006$, with intervals $[-0.0108,-0.0026]$ and $[-0.0013,0.0001]$. Neither a small difference nor an interval crossing zero establishes equivalence. Relevance remains unmeasured.

The three full-translation pilots share the same original 32 IDs and native audit instruction. Direct/placeholder/fixed-block whole-job means are \DirectSeconds{}/\PlaceholderSeconds{}/\BlocksSeconds{} seconds, including generation, checking, and audit where reached.
