\subsection{Joint first-round nomination}
The executable source planner and timings are in \path{benchmarks/plos-joint-byline-depth-v1}. Both repeated runs compare the full ordered read plan against exhaustive pair enumeration at four pool depths, with 1,000 additional seeded equivalence checks. At depth 64, 104 of 160 requests contain a known proof in the union of both pools. Keeping the initial two topical choices fixed and adding one source permits a proof in only 29 requests. The implemented joint policy attains 49 known results under this budget.

The live evaluation in \path{benchmarks/live-joint-source-request-v1} times from request parsing through response serialization with three actual local declaration reads. It includes the query execution audit that explains the eLife cached-to-live difference. Holding the document index fixed, changing the encoding layout changes 71 of 102 eLife source sets at depth 64, 16 of 51 reading plans, and 13 of 51 responses. The fixed-shape encoding audit isolates batch-neighbor dependence on these inputs. \path{benchmarks/selective-joint-topic-check-v1} records equal-issuance comparisons and separates missing topics from demonstrated mistakes. \path{benchmarks/blind-joint-topic-review-v1} documents the pending independent audit.

\path{benchmarks/prose-joint-source-request-v1} tracks known-role, separate, fused, 4B, and guarded interpreter arms for two controlled prose forms. On PLOS, the guard recovers 40 known matches with the same five wrong-role returns as fused interpretation. The eLife guard recovers eight rather than the fused path's ten. The role-gap abstention diagnostic shows that lowering coverage can reduce but does not eliminate wrong-role returns. These experiments hold topic segmentation fixed by grammar.

Loopback service traces appear in \path{benchmarks/joint-loopback-http-v1}. At 272.93 arrivals/s, serial drains at 99.02--100.59 requests/s and dynamic batching at 271.14--271.26. All 844 requests per arm complete in each trace. The high-rate difference is primarily backlog reduction, with end-to-end P95 of 5.06--5.19 s for serial versus 23.49--23.57 ms for dynamic batching. Dynamic batching changes up to five responses against serial at that load. The fixed-shape reference comparison checks byte-level response parity separately.

\paragraph{Queueing under offered load.}
A loopback HTTP replay on the same 59-query SciRepEval panel assigns all policies one GPU worker and two sources per recommendation. Two open-loop traces per rate use identical arrival times and request order across policies. At four requests per second, P95 is 230 ms for length buckets and 201 ms for deferred waves, with all 118 responses per arm within 500 ms. At eight requests per second, immediate waves provide the fastest fixed control at 5.76 drained requests/s and 2.83 s P95. Deferred waves reach 6.47 requests/s and 1.76 s P95. Every reply matches the exhaustive ranking and citations. At this heavier load only 20 of 118 deferred requests finish within 500 ms, versus 14 for each fixed control. These finite traces share a resident model and serialize GPU execution across requests.

On the structured workload served via loopback HTTP, at 58.49 arrivals/s dynamic batching lowers client P95 from 37.59--37.68 to 20.18--20.30 ms, with one response in 844 differing from serial in each run. Fixed-shape batching reproduces its own shape-matched reference, and four of 844 responses differ from serial. At higher rates serial accumulates a backlog. The supplement separates these finite traces from steady-state throughput and external service cost. The batching path is available to all compatible baselines.

\subsection{Scope and personal attribution}
The first study has 168 synthetic views with two sentences and varied measurement scope, conflicts and units. Independent sentence scope checks followed by exact comparison preserve all 36 valid approvals per language and abstain in all 12 conflicting views in each. Their four mistakes per language are unsupported approvals in the absence of a comparable measurement. The second study has 192 views with explicit actor statements. Separate scope and personal-execution checks select no other person's measurements and preserve all 36 valid approvals per language. Every remaining false approval credits the correct person but relies on a scope mismatch. Both studies use frozen Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507. They illustrate why a stronger attribution condition costs time and cannot rescue an incorrect scope decision. The templates supply sentence boundaries and explicit roles.

We then hold a bound fixed and change only the person, project, duty, or measurement status in 480 synthetic cases. Dropping either the person check or the project check causes 48 false approvals, although both ablations solve all 48 intact threshold pairs. Joint binding solves all cases with explicit provenance fields. Rendering the records as prose exposes the evidence selection bottleneck. The 4B selector finds the correct card on 52 of 96 intact requests in plain prose and on 31 with person and project distractors. In the distractor condition, selection followed by arithmetic solves 29 threshold pairs. At least 27 of the corresponding 58 correct decisions use a wrong card. With the correct card, every comparison succeeds. An extra model check leaves 111 false approvals among 384 distractor requests lacking matching evidence and raises local P95 from 24.85 to 39.66 ms. These controls require a separate score for evidence binding.

\subsection{Source selection under missing evidence}
On 246 previously explored author-written LitSearch queries and a 64,183-paper index, dense-gap routing between one and two candidates reduces P95 from 24.93 to 19.79 ms at the 2\% calibration setting. It returns 30 known sources versus 23 for fixed two. When all known targets are removed, the adaptive and fixed routes return six and three unjudged alternatives, respectively. An alternative may still address the question. A blinded review packet has been prepared to adjudicate that distinction.

Across 50 query--facet views in CSFCube's judged pools, the 10\% calibration setting returns 15 strong matches for both gap routing and fixed six. P95 is 23.23 versus 53.90 ms. After stronger candidates are removed, the router returns two grade-zero and four grade-one papers versus two and three for fixed six. At the 20\% setting it returns four fewer strong matches than fixed six. These outcomes compare preset candidate pools, and a shared calibration ceiling does not enforce equal realized error rates.

\paragraph{Automatic role anchors in free text.}
In 36 authored requests with a mandatory condition embedded in the role clause, agreeing model choices bypass the residual proof check on 12 requests. \subsection{Data splits, release, and pending judgments}
The bilingual construction set groups the five query variants under each of 1,000 original needs. It retains all 5,000 pairs in the denominator. A frozen second screening pass retains 4,224 and leaves 776 without a retained screen pass. These are model-screened construction statuses. Expert-fit and independently reviewed semantic labels remain absent. Preserve parent provenance, original offsets, units and comparator direction, and keep real source text outside the public code and aggregate records.

The enterprise scope diagnostic groups 80 development requests by company and lexical near-duplicates before cross-fitting the qualification parser. The resulting 71 groups stay intact. Each fold uses 48 training requests, 16 calibration requests and 16 test requests. The mixed-supervision route skips the 4B parser for 21 requests but changes the qualification set on five of those skips. This is a disagreement with a model reference. Separate freezes retain 154 qualification excerpts for independent judgment and 123 enterprise groups for the predeclared full-index versus shortlist-restricted rescue comparison. Neither review has completed.
